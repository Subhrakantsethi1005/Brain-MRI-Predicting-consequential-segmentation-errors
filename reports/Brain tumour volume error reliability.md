# Conformal RANO triage beats volume correction

**Bottom line:** For a brain-tumour MRI segmentation reliability paper, the 2023–2026 literature has already occupied most of the obvious ground — reference-free case-level QC (overwhelmingly for Dice/overlap, not volume), subject-level uncertainty aggregation for failure detection, conformal *intervals* on tumour volume (Lambert et al. on BraTS 2023), and population-level volume debiasing (Popordanoska 2021, Bertels 2021) — but two clinically meaningful combinations remain genuinely open. Of your two candidates, **(a) reference-free signed-volume CORRECTION is partially taken and the weaker bet**: generic reference-free volume debiasing is prior art, so only the narrow per-case-signed-error-validated-against-a-clinical-threshold framing survives, and it carries a serious circularity/marginality risk because the correcting signal is co-degraded exactly when a correction is most needed ([arXiv 2510.15541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15541)). **(b) A conformal TRIAGE GUARANTEE at the RANO ≥25% threshold is genuinely open**: every ingredient (Conformal Risk Control, case-level error detectors, RANO automation) is published separately, but no paper assembles them into a distribution-free bound on a *case-level* triage decision tied to a named clinical boundary ([conformal_volume note Q4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17897v1)). The single strongest, most defensible, and most achievable contribution is therefore a **conformal case-level progression-reliability flag** — reported as "automatable fraction at a guaranteed missed-consequential-error rate" at the RANO volumetric threshold, built on LUMIERE's longitudinal RANO labels. Its chief risk is that it is an *application/assembly* of mature statistics rather than new theory, which is manageable if the paper anchors on the decision-level recall framing that the literature conspicuously lacks. A handful of flagged citations (notably "Bricout et al. 2026" and the UCSF longitudinal collection specs) must be verified at source before anything is claimed as novel.

## Five reliability sub-fields, and exactly where each is still open

The research question spans five adjacent areas, and the honest "taken vs open" picture differs sharply across them. The table below is the compressed verdict; the prose that follows substantiates the non-obvious calls.

| Area | What is already taken (SOTA) | What is genuinely open |
|---|---|---|
| **(1) Reference-free case-level VOLUME-error triage** | Per-case QC is mature but targets **Dice/overlap** (RCA, Robinson, QU-BraTS, Jungo); the one volume-targeted method predicts *error-bars*, not a triage flag (Eaton-Rosen) | Reference-free case-level flag of **consequential volume error against a clinical threshold**, reported as automatable-fraction-at-fixed-sensitivity — **not claimed** |
| **(2) Confidently-wrong errors invisible to the model's own uncertainty** | Decoupled detectors exist (QCResUNet, "feeling of error", Mahalanobis/kNN OOD, ensemble pairwise-Dice disagreement); Zenk benchmark proves ensembles are jointly, confidently wrong | A detector targeting **signed VOLUME bias** under model confidence, and a clean epistemic-blind-spot-vs-detectable split using **architecturally different** models — **open** |
| **(3) Longitudinal RANO progression-decision reliability + datasets** | Automated longitudinal volumetry tracks burden and beats 2D RANO as a survival surrogate, but best trend agreement is only ~81% and all tools require supervision; **LUMIERE** is the standout longitudinal+RANO dataset | No method models **error correlation across a patient's own serial scans** (compound vs cancel) or outputs a **per-case reliability flag on the RANO decision**; no test-retest CV of the volume *change* — **open** |
| **(4) Post-hoc reference-free volume correction/calibration** | Population-level debiasing is established two ways: calibrate-then-sum (Popordanoska 2021) and regression re-calibration (Bertels 2021), both on BraTS | **Per-case signed-error correction** driven by uncertainty, subtracted, and validated against a **clinical-threshold flip** — not found, but marginal/circular | 
| **(5) Conformal prediction for tumour volume** | **Type A intervals** on volume are occupied: Lambert (BraTS 2023, weighted CP under shift) + ConVOLT (tighter intervals, 2026); evidential calibration (Guennoun, npj 2026) | **Type B** decision/triage guarantees tied to a **case-level** action at **RANO ≥25%** — no paper combines detector + conformal guarantee + clinical threshold — **open** |

The crux running through areas (1), (2) and (4) is that the field's default reliability target is **overlap (Dice/NSD)**, not volume. Reverse Classification Accuracy predicts per-case DSC with no ground truth ([arXiv 1702.03407](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03407)); Robinson et al. predict per-case DSC at MAE 0.03 ([arXiv 1806.06244](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.06244)); QU-BraTS, the brain-tumour uncertainty benchmark, scores voxel-overlap under thresholding and ranks *methods*, not cases ([arXiv 2112.10074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.10074)); QCResUNet predicts subject-level DSC at Pearson r=0.968 but reports **no volume-error metric** ([arXiv 2412.07156](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07156v1)). The single clearest volume-targeted reference-free method, Eaton-Rosen et al., converts MC-dropout uncertainty into **calibrated volumetric confidence intervals** for tumour volume but emits error-bars, not a binary consequential-error flag, and anchors to no clinical threshold ([arXiv 1806.08640](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08640)). This is why area (1)'s precise framing — reference-free, case-level, volume-specific, threshold-anchored, triage-reported — survives the literature intact.

Area (2) is the most intellectually settled: the field has converged on **moving the error signal off the model's own softmax**. The decisive citation is Zenk et al.'s DKFZ benchmark (Jäger/Maier-Hein), which documents that **"the ensemble is overconfident in this case, as the ensemble members agree on the wrongly segmented region"**, finds the naive single-network entropy baseline "performs poorly on most tasks", and shows the best reference-free signal is **inter-prediction disagreement (pairwise Dice)**, with an AURC gap over naive baselines that "can exceed 0.1" — all including BraTS 2019 among its datasets ([arXiv 2406.03323](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03323v1)). Internal-concept detectors ("feeling of error", 2026) attack the plausible-but-wrong case directly on brain-tumour data ([arXiv 2609.08879](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08879)). What remains open is that all of this is overlap/flag-oriented; **no reference-free method outputs a per-case signed volume-error estimate under model confidence** ([confident_error_detection note Q4](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07156v1)).

Area (3) is where the clearest clinical gap sits. Suter et al. (2023) report that even the best longitudinal **trend agreement with a human rater is 81.1% (HD-GLIO volume)** and conclude it is "still insufficient to be used without expert input", with tools fragmenting recurrent tumour along cavity walls ([PMC10513769](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10513769/)). A pediatric DMG study found that despite good single-scan Dice (0.77–0.81), auto-segmented volumes **altered 20% of trajectory-based response labels**, systematically misclassifying stable/progressive disease as partial response via under-segmentation ([irf.fhnw.ch ID#995](https://irf.fhnw.ch/handle/11645/57575)) — empirical evidence that serial errors are *correlated/biased, not random*, which is exactly the signal a decision-level reliability model would exploit. Critically, for 2D RANO **the 25% threshold sits at or below the measurement noise floor**: inter-operator CV runs 44–110% and "tumor measurements producing an increase in bidimensional product of >25% can routinely be obtained solely by chance" ([DOAJ Cancers 2023](https://doaj.org/article/29e4a57ea2cd4f4691686076031d86d4)). Volumetric measurement is more reproducible, which is why Suter operationalised the **volumetric progression threshold at ≥40%** ([PMC10513769](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10513769/)). On data, **LUMIERE is the only public dataset combining many timepoints per patient with expert RANO ratings** — 91 GBM patients, 638 study dates, 616 expert-rated timepoints, with DeepBraTumIA and HD-GLIO-AUTO segmentations, CC BY 4.0 via Figshare ([PMC9755255](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9755255/); [Figshare 5904905](https://figshare.com/collections/The_LUMIERE_Dataset_Longitudinal_Glioblastoma_MRI_with_Expert_RANO_Evaluation/5904905)). RHUH-GBM offers only 3 timepoints/patient under restricted TCIA access ([arXiv 2305.00005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.00005)); BraTS 2024 post-treatment (~2,200 cases, CC BY 4.0, Synapse syn53708249) is effectively cross-sectional ([arXiv 2405.18368](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18368v1)); UCSF-PDGM is single-timepoint preoperative ([arXiv 2109.00356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00356v2)).

Areas (4) and (5) are the most occupied. Generic reference-free volume correction is **prior art**: Popordanoska/Bertels proved that a calibrated-per-image predictor yields the correct volume by summing the soft map, with **|volume bias| ≤ calibration error**, validated on BraTS 2018 glioma ([arXiv 2112.12560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12560)); Bertels' MedIA paper fits a least-squares regression re-calibration of the volume-bias curve ([arXiv 2211.04161](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.04161v1)). For conformal volume, Lambert et al. already own "brain-tumour MRI + conformal volume interval" on **BraTS 2023** with weighted CP under covariate shift ([arXiv 2407.19938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19938)), extended by ConVOLT in 2026 ([arXiv 2603.00798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00798)). The Type A interval space is closed; only the **Type B decision guarantee** remains.

## Verdict on your two candidate contributions

**(a) Reference-free signed-volume CORRECTION — PARTIALLY TAKEN (weak as a headline claim).** The bare concept "turn a raw predicted volume into a debiased volume without test-time ground truth" is squarely prior art, occupied by **Popordanoska/Bertels MICCAI 2021** (calibrate → expected volume, [arXiv 2112.12560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12560)) and **Bertels MedIA 2021** (regression re-calibration, [arXiv 2211.04161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04161)), both validated on BraTS. What is *not* found is the narrow combination: **per-case** signed-error prediction from reference-free signals, subtracted to debias the individual case, and validated against a **clinical decision threshold** ([volume_correction note Q2](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.04161v1)). That narrow version is open, but it carries two documented risks the notes flag explicitly. First, **circularity**: the same soft prediction/uncertainty that produced the volume is used to correct it, so when the network is confidently wrong — the clinically dangerous case — the correcting signal is co-degraded and the correction is smallest exactly where it should be largest; the weak boundary uncertainty-error correlation (r ≈ 0.3 global, ~0 at boundary, [arXiv 2510.15541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15541)) is direct evidence of this failure mode. Second, **marginality**: calibrate-then-sum already captures most recoverable systematic bias, and cross-entropy soft volumes are near-unbiased to begin with, so the headroom is small and noisy ([arXiv 2211.04161](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.04161v1)). This candidate can only be defended if it anchors on *clinical-threshold flips*, not mean ml error, and beats the published comparators (CE soft volume, temperature scaling + expected volume, Bertels regression re-calibration).

**(b) Conformal TRIAGE GUARANTEE at the RANO ≥25% threshold — GENUINELY OPEN.** No paper combines (i) a learned case-level error/failure detector, (ii) a conformal/CRC guarantee on its *triage* decision, and (iii) a tie to a clinical boundary such as RANO ≥25% ([conformal_volume note Q4](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04482v2)). Each ingredient is mature independently: Conformal Risk Control and Learn-then-Test give distribution-free finite-sample risk control ([arXiv 2110.01052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.01052v4)); Conformal Lesion Segmentation guarantees Pr(FNR ≤ ε) ≥ 1−α — but on the **within-image pixel FN fraction**, on 6 CT organs with **no brain** ([arXiv 2510.17897](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17897v1)); CRC-FDR control exists at the pixel/instance level ([arXiv 2504.04482](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.04482v2)); Maganti's deferral removes ~80% of errors at 25% deferral but is pixel-level, on retinal vessels, and explicitly "a post-hoc measurement, not a contractual guarantee" ([arXiv 2604.13262](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13262v1)). The whitespace is the leap from a within-image pixel metric to a **case-level triage recall at a named clinical threshold**. The honest caveat: novelty rests on *assembly + framing*, not new conformal theory, and a reviewer can point to CLS/CRC-FDR as "conformal FNR control for segmentation already done", so the paper must sharply distinguish case/patient-level triage recall from within-image pixel-FNR control.

## Ranked contributions, strongest first

**#1 — Conformal case-level progression-reliability triage at the RANO volumetric threshold (strongest).** *Precise claim:* a reference-free flag that marks a patient's between-timepoint RANO call as "defer to expert", carrying a **distribution-free guarantee that the missed-consequential-error rate ≤ ε** at the RANO threshold (≥25% 2D / ≥40% volumetric), reported as the *fraction of RANO decisions safely automatable at that guaranteed sensitivity*. *Why novel:* occupies the one cell no cited paper fills — Type B, case-level, at a clinical boundary — whereas CLS/CRC control pixel masks ([arXiv 2510.17897](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17897v1)), Lambert gives intervals not decisions ([arXiv 2407.19938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19938)), and Maganti gives no guarantee ([arXiv 2604.13262](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13262v1)). *Honest risk:* it is an application of mature CRC/LTT, not new theory; LUMIERE's 616 rated timepoints may be too few to split into a calibration set that yields a tight ε at useful α, and exchangeability is violated across sites (Lambert's weighted CP would be needed). *Feasibility on a 6GB laptop:* high — the heavy segmentation masks already ship with LUMIERE (DeepBraTumIA, HD-GLIO-AUTO), so the work is calibration and detector-fitting on precomputed volumes, not training a 3D segmenter. *Data:* **LUMIERE (longitudinal, required)** — the only public source with per-timepoint expert RANO labels ([PMC9755255](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9755255/)).

**#2 — Empirical noise floor of the automated between-timepoint VOLUME CHANGE vs the RANO threshold.** *Precise claim:* the first published test-retest / serial-scan CV of the automated *delta* volume for glioma, positioned against the 25%/40% RANO thresholds, with a model of whether per-scan errors **compound or cancel** in the difference. *Why novel:* no paper reports the repeatability of the change measurement (only single-scan correlations and aggregate ~81% trend agreement); error-correlation across a patient's own scans is unmodelled ([longitudinal_rano note Q2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10513769/)), and the pediatric DMG result shows the errors are biased, not random ([irf.fhnw.ch ID#995](https://irf.fhnw.ch/handle/11645/57575)). *Honest risk:* descriptive/measurement contribution — may read as an analysis rather than a method unless paired with #1; needs enough repeat or closely-spaced scans to estimate a CV. *Feasibility:* very high (pure analysis of existing masks). *Data:* **LUMIERE (longitudinal, required)**; RHUH-GBM as a short pre/post/recurrence check.

**#3 — A signed-volume-error detector/corrector for the confident-error regime, validated by threshold flips.** *Precise claim:* a per-case estimator of signed volume bias built on an **independent/disagreement signal** (e.g. architecturally different second model, or internal-concept probe) rather than the model's own softmax, subtracted and judged by how often it prevents an erroneous RANO threshold crossing. *Why novel:* combines your candidate (a) with the area (2) insight that decoupled signals catch what uncertainty cannot ([arXiv 2406.03323](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03323v1)); no reference-free method outputs a per-case signed volume error validated against a clinical flip ([volume_correction note Q2](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.04161v1)). *Honest risk:* the circularity/marginality problem above — gains may be small ml and dominated by the published calibrate-then-sum baseline; must beat it on threshold flips, not mean error. *Feasibility:* high on BraTS (cross-sectional training of the detector); medium if it must also run longitudinally. *Data:* **BraTS 2023 (already downloaded)** for detector training; **LUMIERE** for the threshold-flip validation.

**#4 — An epistemic-blind-spot vs detectable-error partition for brain-tumour volume, using architecturally distinct models.** *Precise claim:* quantify the fraction of consequential volume errors that are invisible to *any* homogeneous-ensemble uncertainty but recoverable by an architecturally independent cross-check. *Why novel:* the benchmark uses disagreement among *homogeneous* models and never partitions errors this way for brain tumour ([confident_error_detection note Q3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03323v1)). *Honest risk:* more analysis than method; defining "architecturally different" rigorously is tricky; may overlap with the "feeling of error" line if that paper's full results are strong. *Feasibility:* medium (needs two trained segmenters). *Data:* **BraTS 2023 (already downloaded)**.

## Conclusion

The decisive asymmetry between your two candidates is that **volume correction competes against published baselines on a metric (mean volume bias) where the headroom is small and the signal self-degrades in the dangerous cases, while the conformal RANO triage guarantee competes against nobody at the case-decision level and rides on a clinical fact — that the 25% threshold sits inside the measurement noise band — that makes the whole exercise clinically necessary rather than merely technically clever.** The reliability field has spent 2023–2026 proving that the model's own confidence is untrustworthy (Zenk, Wong, Maganti) and that calibration gains don't translate to better decisions; the natural next move the literature has not made is to stop reporting aggregate trend-agreement and instead attach a *guaranteed* reliability contract to the individual progression call. That is the paper to write, and its feasibility is unusually favourable: LUMIERE ships the segmentations, so a 6GB laptop never has to train a 3D network, only calibrate a guarantee on precomputed volumes. The strategic recommendation is to lead with contribution #1, use contribution #2 as its empirical foundation (the noise floor that motivates why automation is unsafe without a guarantee), and treat the signed-volume corrector (your candidate a, contribution #3) as a secondary comparator rather than the headline — reframed, not abandoned, because its honest value is as the thing a conformal triage layer wraps, not as a standalone novelty.
