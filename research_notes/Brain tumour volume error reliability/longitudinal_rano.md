# Longitudinal glioma volumetry reliability, RANO progression assessment, and longitudinal datasets (2023-2026)

Scope note: covers (1) automated longitudinal volumetry vs RANO/RANO 2.0/mRANO, (2) whether anyone models how per-scan segmentation errors compound/cancel in the between-timepoint difference or triages unreliable progression calls, (3) known inter-rater / test-retest VOLUME variability and where the 25% threshold sits, (4) multi-timepoint glioma datasets. Foundational RANO (Macdonald 1990; Wen 2010) is background; prioritised 2023-2026.

## Q1: Automated longitudinal tumour volumetry and agreement with RANO / RANO 2.0 / mRANO; error of automatic CHANGE measurement vs manual

### Takeaway
Several 2019-2024 papers show automated volumetric segmentation tracks longitudinal tumour burden and even outperforms manual bidimensional RANO as a survival surrogate, BUT the best longitudinal *trend* agreement with a human rater is only ~81%, and authors uniformly conclude the tools are NOT reliable enough to run unsupervised. RANO 2.0 (2023) still makes 2D the primary measurement and only *permits* volumetrics as an option. No paper reports a validated per-decision reliability/error on the change measurement itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Kickingereder et al. 2019 (Lancet Oncology), multicentre retrospective ANN response assessment**: ANN produced volumetric segmentation in <10 min without manual intervention; disagreement between time-to-progression (TTP) from manual vs automatic segmentations was **13%**, substantially lower than the **49%** disagreement between local and central RANO assessment; volume-derived TTP was a better surrogate for overall survival than central RANO — [Healio summary of Kickingereder Lancet Oncol 2019](https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20190624/artificial-neural-network-shows-potential-for-assessment-of-glioblastoma-treatment-response)
- **Suter et al. 2023, "Evaluating automated longitudinal tumor measurements for glioblastoma response assessment" (Frontiers in Radiology, doi:10.3389/fradi.2023.1211859)**: 80 GBM patients, 502 post-operative timepoints (129 timepoints had measurable target lesions). Compared two tools (BraTumIA, HD-GLIO) vs expert RANO. Thresholds used: **≥25% increase for 2D, ≥40% increase for volumetric**. Tumour-volume TREND agreement with expert 2D SPD: **HD-GLIO 81.1%, BraTumIA 79.7%**. Spearman correlation of tool volume to expert: HD-GLIO 0.97, BraTumIA 0.89. Best TTP match (BraTumIA volume) was -9.75 days mean diff with a 144.5-day IQR. HD-GLIO 2D SPD gave highest TTP-OS correlation 0.55 (vs expert TTP-OS 0.40) — [PMC10513769](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10513769/)
- Suter et al. explicit conclusion: **"Even the highest reported trend agreement with the human rater of 81.1% (HD-GLIO volume) is still insufficient to be used without expert input."** Tools failed to track non-measurable lesions and fragmented recurrent tumour along cavity walls into multiple lesions, distorting longitudinal measurements — [PMC10513769](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10513769/)
- **Deep-learning AutoRANO pipeline (arXiv:2209.01402)**: end-to-end tool segments enhancing tumour, edema, surgical cavity, then computes bidimensional + volumetric RANO measurements; validated on 760 pre-op and 504 post-op patients (19 public sites 2020-2021; trial sites 2009-2011). Reports "high agreement" for delta-RANO vs expert raters; authors state multicentre clinical-trial validation still needed before implementation — [arXiv:2209.01402](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.01402)
- **RANO 2.0 (Wen et al. 2023, J Clin Oncol 41:5187-5199)**: unified criteria for high- and low-grade gliomas; uses **post-radiotherapy MRI as baseline** (not post-surgical) in newly diagnosed setting; **primary measurement remains 2D maximum cross-sectional area, volumetrics an OPTION**; requires confirmation of progression within 12 weeks of RT due to pseudoprogression; for IDH-mutant tumours may evaluate both enhancing and non-enhancing components — [Wen et al. 2023 full text PDF](https://www.brainlife.org/fulltext/2023/Wen_PY230929_JClinOncol.pdf)

### Inferences
- The field consistently reports volumetric TTP as a *better survival surrogate* than 2D RANO, yet no one publishes the measurement error of the *between-timepoint volume change* (the actual progression input) with its own confidence interval — they report single-scan correlations and trend-agreement percentages instead.
- "Trend agreement" (~80%) is already a decision-level metric (did the tool and human agree on the direction of change), implying ~1 in 5 longitudinal calls disagrees — but this is reported as an aggregate, not flagged per case.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports the repeatability/error specifically of the automated DELTA (change) measurement with a confidence interval, as opposed to the single-scan volume.

## Q2: Do per-scan errors COMPOUND or CANCEL in the between-timepoint difference? Anyone triage/flag unreliable progression calls? (DECISION-level reliability)

### Takeaway
This is the clearest GAP and supports the candidate novelty. Work exists on (a) longitudinal temporal inconsistency of segmentation tools, (b) per-scan segmentation uncertainty quantification, and (c) selective-prediction/abstention in medical imaging generally — but I found NO glioma paper that explicitly models error CORRELATION across a patient's own scans (compounding vs cancelling in the difference) or that outputs a per-case reliability flag on the PROGRESSION/RANO decision itself. The closest evidence is a pediatric-glioma abstract showing segmentation errors flip 20% of longitudinal response labels.

### Cited Findings
- **Pediatric diffuse midline glioma (DMG) abstract, "Automated segmentation performance and uncertainty…" (Neuro-Oncology / neuped, ID #995, 2024-2025)**: despite good single-scan Dice (median 0.77-0.81), auto-segmented volumes **altered 20% of trajectory-based manual response labels**, predominantly misclassifying stable/progressive disease as partial response due to UNDER-segmentation of post-treatment scans; segmentation error correlated (|r|=0.9) with human contour uncertainty — [irf.fhnw.ch ID#995](https://irf.fhnw.ch/handle/11645/57575) ; [citedrive record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/id-995-automated-segmentation-performance-and-uncertainty-in-pediatric-diffuse-midline-gliomas-using-imaging-biomarkers/)
- **"Unstable Prompts, Unreliable Segmentations: A Challenge for Longitudinal Lesion Analysis" (arXiv:2507.19230, 2025)**: documents that automated/promptable tools struggle with TEMPORAL CONSISTENCY across timepoints; universal lesion-segmentation models are built for single timepoints — frames the longitudinal-consistency problem but does not provide a decision-reliability model — [arXiv:2507.19230](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.19230)
- **Rel-UNet: Reliable Tumor Segmentation via Uncertainty Quantification in nnU-Net (arXiv:2503.09633, 2025)**: per-scan uncertainty quantification to flag unreliable segmentations; single-timepoint, not decision-level/longitudinal — [arXiv:2503.09633](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.09633)
- **Selective prediction / abstention (general medical imaging, 2025-2026)**: "learning with a reject option" lets a model abstain on inputs it deems unreliable and defer to a human; established in medical imaging for single diagnoses. Note cautionary result: when told the AI abstained, clinicians underdiagnosed (+18% missed diagnoses) and undertreated (+35% missed treatments) vs no AI — [On the Limits of Selective AI Prediction, arXiv:2508.07617](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07617v1) ; [DEGRE, AAAI 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/42473)

### Inferences
- The triage/selective framing is mature in single-scan classification/segmentation but has NOT been transferred to the between-timepoint PROGRESSION decision in glioma. Modelling whether per-scan errors are correlated (systematic under-segmentation at both timepoints would partly CANCEL in the difference; independent errors would COMPOUND) appears genuinely unaddressed — the pediatric DMG result (systematic under-segmentation flipping labels one direction) is empirical evidence that errors are correlated/biased, not random, which is directly relevant.
- A reliability flag on the RANO call (rather than on a single mask) is a defensible novel contribution; existing tools report only aggregate trend-agreement.

### Gaps
- No glioma paper found that (a) estimates error correlation across a patient's own serial scans, or (b) predicts per-case whether a RANO progression/response call is reliable. This is the central unmet need the candidate project targets.

## Q3: Inter-rater / test-retest VOLUME variability for glioma, and where the 25% threshold sits

### Takeaway
RANO's bidimensional measurement has poor-to-moderate inter-operator reproducibility, and intra-observer variability is large enough that a >25% bidimensional increase can be produced by chance alone — i.e. the 25% progression threshold sits AT OR BELOW the measurement noise floor for 2D. Volumetric measurement is more reproducible (which is why RANO 2.0 raises the volumetric progression threshold to ~40%), but I did not find a single clean glioma test-retest volume CV number; general brain-volume test-retest CVs are ~1.6-6%.

### Cited Findings
- **Evaluation of RANO criteria (Cancers, Jun 2023; see also Hales/UCL and related)**: RANO bidimensional product measurements had **poor-to-moderate inter-operator reproducibility (r²=0.28-0.82; CV 44-110%; mean % diff 0.4-46.8%)** and moderate-to-excellent intra-operator reproducibility (r²=0.71-0.88; CV 31-58%; diff 0.3-23.9%). **"Tumor measurements producing an increase in bidimensional product of >25% can routinely be obtained solely by chance"** given intra-observer variability. Accuracy of RANO vs 2D ground truth was 66.7%/65.1% (AUC 0.67/0.66); vs volumetric ground truth only 21.0%/56.5% (AUC 0.39/0.55) — [DOAJ Cancers 2023](https://doaj.org/article/29e4a57ea2cd4f4691686076031d86d4) ; [Hales UCL AAM PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10069606/1/Hales%20AAM%201NRAD-D-17-00403_R2%5B1%5D.pdf)
- **RANO 2.0 thresholds**: 2D progression threshold ≥25%; the Suter 2023 study operationalised the **volumetric progression threshold at ≥40% increase** (reflecting the larger dynamic range / better reproducibility of volume) — [PMC10513769](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10513769/)
- **General brain-volume test-retest (Scientific Data)**: CV of volumetric measurements ranged **1.6% (caudate) to 6.1% (thalamus)** with repositioning between scans — a lower bound for well-defined structures, not tumour — [Scientific Data test-retest, link.springer sdata.2014.37](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/sdata.2014.37)
- **rCBV/DCE repeatability in glioma** (context, not volume): tumour standardized rCBV within-subject CV 13-20%; DCE Ktrans/ve CV ~6-8% — [PMC4411010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4411010)

### Inferences
- For 2D RANO the 25% threshold is inside the noise band (CV routinely exceeds 25%), so a meaningful fraction of "progression" calls are measurement artefact — strong motivation for a reliability triage.
- Volumetric segmentation is more reproducible, but the relevant quantity is the test-retest of the *volume difference* between two scans for the SAME patient/tool, which I could not find quantified for glioma.

### Gaps
- No clean glioma-specific automated-segmentation test-retest volume CV, and specifically no test-retest of the between-timepoint volume CHANGE. This is a quantitative gap the candidate project could fill and cite against the 25%/40% thresholds.

## Q4: Longitudinal / multi-timepoint glioma MRI datasets (name, #cases, timepoints, modalities, annotations, licence, access)

### Takeaway
LUMIERE is the standout genuinely-longitudinal dataset WITH expert RANO ratings. RHUH-GBM is longitudinal but only 3 timepoints/patient. BraTS 2024 post-treatment is the largest annotated post-treatment set but is effectively cross-sectional (single timepoint per case). UCSF-PDGM is preoperative single-timepoint. A UCSF "Longitudinal Post-Treatment Diffuse Glioma" collection is referenced but I lack verified specs.

### Cited Findings
- **LUMIERE (Suter et al., Scientific Data, Dec 2022; s41597-022-01881-7)**: **91 GBM patients, 638 study dates, 2,487 images, 616 expert-rated timepoints** (truly longitudinal, many timepoints/patient). Expert neuroradiologist (14 yrs) RANO ratings: progressive disease / stable / partial response / complete response, WITH rationale and bidimensional Macdonald measurements. Modalities: T1, T1-post-contrast (CT1), T2, FLAIR. Provided automated segmentations from **DeepBraTumIA** (necrosis, enhancement, edema) and **HD-GLIO-AUTO** (enhancing tumour, T2 abnormality); PyRadiomics + CoLlAGe radiomics; MGMT (37 methylated/43 unmethylated/11 unknown), IDH1, overall survival. **Licence CC BY 4.0, non-commercial; access via Figshare** — [PMC9755255](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9755255/) ; [Figshare collection 5904905](https://figshare.com/collections/The_LUMIERE_Dataset_Longitudinal_Glioblastoma_MRI_with_Expert_RANO_Evaluation/5904905)
- **RHUH-GBM (Cepeda et al., Scientific Data 2023; arXiv:2305.00005)**: **40 WHO grade-4 patients, 400 studies — 3 timepoints each: preoperative, early post-operative, and follow-up at recurrence** (longitudinal but short). Multiparametric MRI, expert-CORRECTED tumour-subregion segmentations, molecular data, survival. Río Hortega University Hospital, Valladolid, Spain. **Published on TCIA under controlled/restricted access** — [arXiv:2305.00005](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.00005) ; [PMC10551826](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10551826)
- **BraTS 2024 post-treatment glioma ("BraTS-PTG"/post-treatment track) (de Verdier et al., arXiv:2405.18368)**: **~2,200 MRI cases from 7 US+Heidelberg centres** (Duke 680, UCSF 600, Missouri 400, UC San Diego 350, Heidelberg 300, Michigan 100, Indiana 70). Four annotated subregions: **enhancing tissue (ET), non-enhancing T2/FLAIR hyperintensity (SNFH), non-enhancing tumour core (NETC), resection cavity (RC)**. Split 70% train / 10% val / 20% test; only training has public labels. **Effectively cross-sectional (not described as multi-timepoint per patient).** Licence **CC BY 4.0**; hosted on Synapse syn53708249 — [arXiv:2405.18368](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18368v1) ; [Synapse syn53708249](https://www.synapse.org/#!Synapse:syn53708249/wiki/627500)
- **UCSF-PDGM (Calabrese et al., Radiology:AI 2022; arXiv:2109.00356)**: **500 subjects, preoperative, SINGLE timepoint** (not longitudinal). Standardised 3T protocol with 3D, diffusion (HARDI) and perfusion (ASL); tumour segmentations; IDH status all cases, MGMT for WHO III/IV. 11,523 images, 156.5 GB, NIfTI, skull-stripped/deidentified. **TCIA, DOI 10.7937/tcia.bdgf-8v37** — [TCIA UCSF-PDGM](https://wiki.cancerimagingarchive.net/x/TgOcBw) ; [arXiv:2109.00356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00356v2)
- Note: a **UCSF "Longitudinal Post-Treatment Diffuse Glioma"** collection is referenced as a contributing source within the BraTS 2024 effort — [arXiv:2405.18368](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18368v1)

### Inferences
- For studying DECISION-level progression reliability, **LUMIERE is the primary usable public resource** (many timepoints/patient + expert RANO labels + two tool segmentations enabling tool-vs-expert and tool-vs-tool error analysis). RHUH-GBM supports a pre/post/recurrence delta but only one progression event. BraTS-2024-PTG and UCSF-PDGM are best for training/validating per-scan segmentation, not for longitudinal decision reliability.

### Gaps
- Verified specs (case count, #timepoints, access/licence) for the UCSF "Longitudinal Post-Treatment Diffuse Glioma" collection were not confirmed.
- Did not confirm whether any TCIA collection beyond RHUH-GBM/UCSF provides serial post-treatment timepoints WITH per-timepoint RANO/response labels (LUMIERE remains the only one found with explicit expert RANO ratings).

## Explicit statement of what is NOT yet done (longitudinal reliability/triage)
- No glioma method models error CORRELATION across a patient's own serial scans (whether per-scan segmentation errors compound or cancel in the between-timepoint difference).
- No method outputs a per-case reliability/abstention flag on the PROGRESSION (RANO/response) decision; existing reliability work is single-scan (segmentation uncertainty) or aggregate (trend-agreement %).
- No published test-retest CV of the automated between-timepoint VOLUME CHANGE for glioma, so there is no empirical noise floor to compare against the 25% (2D) / 40% (volumetric) RANO thresholds.
- Selective-prediction/abstention is established for single diagnoses/segmentations but not transferred to the serial-decision setting in neuro-oncology.
